## 1. Digital Signal Processing & Audio Sampling

### A. Continuous to Discrete Transformation
Continuous acoustic speech $x_c(t)$ generated by vocal tract resonance is sampled by an analog-to-digital converter (ADC) at a constant sampling frequency $f_s$:
$$x[n] = x_c(n T_s) = x_c\left(\frac{n}{f_s}\right), \quad n \in \mathbb{Z}$$
where $T_s = \frac{1}{f_s}$ is the sampling period.

### B. Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Theorem & Aliasing
To prevent irreversible spectral distortion (*aliasing*), the sampling frequency $f_s$ must exceed twice the highest frequency component $B$ present in the signal:
$$f_s > 2B$$
When downsampling from $f_{\text{orig}} = 48,000\text{ Hz}$ to $f_{\text{target}} = 16,000\text{ Hz}$ in Trustwire, the continuous spectrum of the discrete sequence contains periodic repetitions:
$$X_{\text{sampled}}(f) = f_s \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} X\left(f - k f_s\right)$$
- If the signal contains energy above the new Nyquist frequency $f_{\text{Nyquist}} = \frac{16000}{2} = 8,000\text{ Hz}$, higher frequencies fold back into audible frequencies ($f_{\text{aliased}} = |f - f_s|$).
- **Implementation Guarantee:** Trustwire's PyAV `AudioResampler` applies an 8th-order low-pass anti-aliasing sinc filter with cutoff at $7,800\text{ Hz}$ prior to decimation, eliminating spurious high-frequency artifacts.

## 2. Time-Frequency Transformations: STFT & Mel Scale

### A. Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)
To capture non-stationary phoneme transitions over time, the signal $x[n]$ is windowed into localized segments:
$$X(m, k) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n + mH] \cdot w[n] \cdot e^{-j \frac{2\pi}{N} k n}$$
where:
- $m \in \mathbb{Z}$: Time frame index.
- $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$: Discrete frequency bin index.
- $N$: Analysis frame length (typically 512 or 1024 samples).
- $H$: Hop length (stride between successive windows, e.g., 256 samples).
- $w[n]$: Symmetric smoothing window (Hann or Hamming), mitigating spectral leakage:
  $$w_{\text{Hann}}[n] = 0.5 \left(1 - \cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{N-1}\right)\right), \quad 0 \le n \le N-1$$

### B. Power Spectrogram & Decibel Scaling
The complex STFT coefficient $X(m, k) = A + jB$ yields the power spectrogram:
$$S(m, k) = |X(m, k)|^2 = (\text{Re}\{X(m,k)\})^2 + (\text{Im}\{X(m,k)\})^2$$
Expressed on the logarithmic decibel (dB) scale:
$$S_{\text{dB}}(m, k) = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{S(m, k) + \epsilon}{P_{\text{ref}}}\right)$$
where $\epsilon = 10^{-10}$ prevents $\log(0)$ singularities.

### C. The Mel Scale & Filterbank Projection
Human auditory perception is logarithmic—more sensitive to small pitch variations at low frequencies than at high frequencies. The Mel scale models this psychoacoustic non-linearity:
$$m = 2595 \log_{10}\left(1 + \frac{f}{700}\right)$$
Inverse conversion back to Hertz:
$$f = 700 \left(10^{\frac{m}{2595}} - 1\right)$$
A triangular filterbank $\{H_m(k)\}_{m=1}^M$ computes Mel energies:
$$\text{MelEnergy}(m) = \sum_{k=0}^{N/2} S(k) \cdot H_m(k)$$

## 3. Neural Classifier Formulations

### A. Softmax Posterior Probability
Given an unnormalized logit vector $\mathbf{z} = [z_0, z_1]^T \in \mathbb{R}^2$ output by a neural classification head, the posterior probabilities are computed via the canonical Softmax operator:
$$P(Y = i \mid \mathbf{x}) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=0}^{1} e^{z_j}}, \quad i \in \{0, 1\}$$
For binary classification where Class 0 = Real and Class 1 = Fake:
$$P(\text{Fake}) = \frac{e^{z_1}}{e^{z_0} + e^{z_1}} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(z_1 - z_0)}} = \sigma(z_1 - z_0)$$
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the standard logistic sigmoid function.

### B. Binary Cross-Entropy Loss (BCE)
During offline model training, the network parameters $\theta$ are optimized by minimizing binary cross-entropy:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^M \left[ y_i \log(\hat{y}_i) + (1 - y_i) \log(1 - \hat{y}_i) \right]$$
where $y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is ground-truth authenticity and $\hat{y}_i = P(\text{Fake} \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$.

## 4. Vision Transformer (ViT) Mathematical Formulation

### A. Patch Partitioning & Linear Projection
An input face image $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ (where $H=224, W=224, C=3$) is flattened into a sequence of $N$ non-overlapping 2D patches $\mathbf{x}_p \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (P^2 C)}$:
$$P = 16 \implies N = \frac{H \cdot W}{P^2} = \frac{224 \times 224}{16 \times 16} = 14 \times 14 = 196\text{ patches}$$
Each patch has dimension $P^2 C = 16 \times 16 \times 3 = 768$.

A trainable linear projection matrix $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{(P^2 C) \times D}$ (with embedding dimension $D = 768$) maps patches into latent vector representations:
$$\mathbf{z}_0 = \left[ \mathbf{x}_{\text{class}}; \; \mathbf{x}_p^1 \mathbf{E}; \; \mathbf{x}_p^2 \mathbf{E}; \; \dots; \; \mathbf{x}_p^N \mathbf{E} \right] + \mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}}$$
where:
- $\mathbf{x}_{\text{class}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is the learnable classification token `[CLS]`.
- $\mathbf{E}_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+1) \times D}$ denotes learned 1D spatial position embeddings.

### B. Scaled Dot-Product Self-Attention
For a sequence of query, key, and value matrices $\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+1) \times d_k}$:
$$\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}$$

#### Mathematical Proof: Why the Scaling Factor $\sqrt{d_k}$ is Essential
Assume the components of $\mathbf{q}$ and $\mathbf{k}$ are independent random variables with zero mean and unit variance:
$$\mathbb{E}[q_i] = 0, \quad \text{Var}(q_i) = 1, \quad \mathbb{E}[k_i] = 0, \quad \text{Var}(k_i) = 1$$
The dot product is given by $S = \mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{k} = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i$.
1. **Mean of the dot product:**
   $$\mathbb{E}[S] = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} \mathbb{E}[q_i k_i] = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} \mathbb{E}[q_i]\mathbb{E}[k_i] = 0$$
2. **Variance of the dot product:**
   $$\text{Var}(S) = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} \text{Var}(q_i k_i) = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} \left( \mathbb{E}[q_i^2 k_i^2] - (\mathbb{E}[q_i k_i])^2 \right)$$
   $$\text{Since } \mathbb{E}[q_i^2] = \text{Var}(q_i) = 1 \text{ and } \mathbb{E}[k_i^2] = 1:$$
   $$\text{Var}(S) = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} (1 \cdot 1 - 0) = d_k$$
For large embedding dimensions (e.g., $d_k = 64$), the variance of the dot product grows to 64, meaning values of $S$ can easily reach $\pm 16$. When inputs to the Softmax function are very large, Softmax saturates, yielding gradients approaching zero ($\frac{\partial \text{softmax}}{\partial z} \approx 0$), halting backpropagation.
By dividing by $\sqrt{d_k} = \sqrt{64} = 8$:
$$\text{Var}\left(\frac{\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{k}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) = \frac{\text{Var}(S)}{d_k} = \frac{d_k}{d_k} = 1$$
This enforces unit variance across attention matrices, stabilizing gradients throughout all 12 transformer layers.

## 5. Normalized Cross-Correlation (Template Matching)

For multi-scale Google SynthID watermark detection in `backend/detectors/video/detector.py`:
$$R(x, y) = \frac{\sum_{x', y'} \left( T'(x', y') \cdot I'(x + x', y + y') \right)}{\sqrt{ \sum_{x', y'} \left( T'(x', y') \right)^2 \cdot \sum_{x', y'} \left( I'(x + x', y + y') \right)^2 }}$$
where:
- $T'(x', y') = T(x', y') - \bar{T}$ (mean-subtracted template).
- $I'(x+x', y+y') = I(x+x', y+y') - \bar{I}_{x,y}$ (mean-subtracted local image patch).
- Cauchy-Schwarz inequality guarantees $R(x, y) \in [-1.0, 1.0]$.
- $R(x, y) = 1.0$ represents a mathematically perfect edge correlation match.

## 6. Financial Scam Context-Aware Compounding Equation

In `backend/fraud/engine.py`, the cumulative risk score $S_{\text{scam}}$ across matched phrases is calculated using an additive base with a non-linear compounding multiplier based on the number of distinct threat categories $C$:
$$S_{\text{scam}} = \min\left( 1.0, \; \left( \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i}{100.0} \right) \cdot \left( 1.0 + 0.30 \cdot (C - 1) \right) \right)$$
where:
- $w_i \in [10, 45]$: Individual rule weights assigned to matched regex patterns.
- $C = |\{\text{Category}(i) : i \in \text{Matches}\}|$: Total count of distinct matched fraud categories ($C \ge 1$).
- If educational/news keywords (e.g. *"cybersecurity awareness"*, *"warning to public"*) are present, a 60% suppression dampener is applied:
  $$S_{\text{dampened}} = S_{\text{scam}} \times 0.40$$

### Worked Numerical Example:
Suppose a malicious call contains two matched patterns:
1. "Urgent wire transfer required immediately" (Category: `urgent_action`, Weight: $w_1 = 35$)
2. "Share your one-time password OTP code" (Category: `credential_theft`, Weight: $w_2 = 40$)
- Sum of weights: $\sum w_i = 35 + 40 = 75$.
- Distinct categories: $C = 2$ (`urgent_action`, `credential_theft`).
- Multiplier: $1.0 + 0.30 \times (2 - 1) = 1.30$.
- Composite Score:
  $$S = \min\left(1.0, \; \frac{75}{100} \times 1.30\right) = \min(1.0, \; 0.75 \times 1.30) = 0.975\text{ (97.5% - Critical Severity Alert)}$$

## 7. Statistical Evaluation Metrics

For forensic benchmarking, classification results are evaluated against ground truth using standard binary confusion matrix formulations:

| | Predicted Authentic ($P=0$) | Predicted Synthetic ($P=1$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Actual Authentic ($Y=0$)** | True Negative ($TN$) | False Positive ($FP$) |
| **Actual Synthetic ($Y=1$)** | False Negative ($FN$) | True Positive ($TP$) |

### Metric Formulations:
1. **Accuracy:**
   $$\text{ACC} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}$$
2. **Precision (Positive Predictive Value):**
   $$\text{PPV} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$$
3. **Recall (Sensitivity / True Positive Rate):**
   $$\text{TPR} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$$
4. **Specificity (True Negative Rate):**
   $$\text{TNR} = \frac{TN}{TN + FP}$$
5. **False Positive Rate (Fall-out):**
   $$\text{FPR} = \frac{FP}{TN + FP} = 1 - \text{Specificity}$$
6. **F1-Score (Harmonic Mean of Precision and Recall):**
   $$F_1 = 2 \cdot \frac{\text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}} = \frac{2TP}{2TP + FP + FN}$$
7. **Equal Error Rate (EER):** The specific decision threshold $\theta^*$ at which the False Acceptance Rate ($\text{FAR} = \text{FPR}$) equals the False Rejection Rate ($\text{FRR} = \text{FNR}$):
   $$\text{EER} = \text{FAR}(\theta^*) = \text{FRR}(\theta^*)$$
